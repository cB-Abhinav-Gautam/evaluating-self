Pairings

Two sources from different traditions that turn out to make the same claim, read together: the claim they share, the evidence on each side, and the point where the likeness breaks. The ring shows where the pairings run: each ribbon joins two questions and is as thick as the number of pairings between them.

AI Snake Oil × Bainbridge

How to get good output

AI Snake Oil, Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor

overautomation, and human oversight as the fine print under a full-automation pitch

Ironies of Automation, Lisanne Bainbridge

the human left to supervise a process they can no longer perform

Shared claim

An oversight role that exists on paper stops being exercised in practice.

Evidence

Toronto's beach model, never once overruled while beaches stayed open on 64 per cent of unsafe days; the Dutch childcare benefit system and about thirty thousand wrongly accused parents.

Evidence

The account of monitoring and manual skill decaying through disuse.

Where it breaks

Bainbridge describes a skill decaying over time, which implies it was once there. The Toronto and Dutch cases show the override unused from the first day, which makes it an institutional fact rather than a human-factors one, and training will not touch it.

AI Snake Oil × Christian

the target variable is not the thing it stands for, and the substitution carries the inequality in

The Alignment Problem, Brian Christian

the alignment problem: systems optimise what they were given

Shared claim

A system optimises the variable it was given, and the harm lives in the gap between that variable and what anyone meant.

Evidence

Optum's Impact Pro predicting spending rather than need; COMPAS developers speaking of crime while the data records arrests.

Evidence

The book-length case that specification is where the failures originate.

Where it breaks

Christian treats this as a specification problem with technical fixes arriving. Narayanan and Kapoor treat the gap as constitutive in social prediction, because need, crime and merit have no recordable proxy at all, so a better optimiser makes it worse rather than better.

AI Snake Oil × Epley

How to get good output · Who is doing the work

AI Snake Oil, Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor

individual social outcomes cannot be predicted well enough to decide from

Mindwise, Nicholas Epley

stereotypes get the direction of a group difference right and the magnitude wrong

Shared claim

A judgment about an individual assembled from group regularities misses the thing that actually decided the case.

Evidence

The Fragile Families Challenge: 160 teams and over 10,000 variables per child, and nothing beat a baseline of four.

Evidence

The wealth-inequality survey, where a measured party gap of 3.5 points was predicted at 35 and a rich–poor gap of 3 at 40.

Where it breaks

Epley's remedy is to ask the person, and a decision system has nobody to ask: in the Fragile Families case no question existed that would have caught the neighbour who counselled the child and fed him blueberries. The error is the same shape; only one side has a fix.

Aguera y Arcas × Anthropic Biology

How to know it is right

What Is Intelligence?

the interpreter: the part that speaks composes an account of a decision it did not make

On the Biology of a Large Language Model, Jack Lindsey and colleagues

written reasoning assembled backwards from a conclusion, traced in the circuits that produced it

Shared claim

An explanation produced after a decision is fluent whether or not it is the reason, and the one producing it cannot tell the difference.

Evidence

Gazzaniga's split-brain patient explaining that the shovel is for the chicken shed; Johansson's swapped photographs, noticed by thirteen per cent, with justifications for choices never made indistinguishable from real ones; the 2010 Swedish election run, where ninety-two per cent endorsed altered survey answers as their own.

Evidence

the cosine prompt where a suggested value becomes the target and the written steps are built back from it, with the suggested number moved and the reasoning following it.

Where it breaks

The model can be caught. Its circuits are readable, and the backwards assembly shows up as a graph that does not contain the procedure the model describes. No such trace exists for the person, so the only available check on a human account is an outcome measured outside the head. The causes differ too: the chapter blamed the model's confabulation on having no state between tokens, and models that keep a scratchpad do it anyway, while the human interpreter has continuous state and confabulates regardless — which means statelessness was never the mechanism on either side.

Aguera y Arcas × Sarvapriyananda

Who is doing the work

What Is Intelligence?

a self is whatever keeps a boundary and predicts across it, and it arrives with nobody putting it there

Consciousness Across Three Worldviews, Sarvapriyananda, Agüera y Arcas and Rovelli

an individual self begins with a distinction drawn, physical at the membrane and conceptual at the umwelt

Shared claim

Selfhood is not a substance added to a system; it is what a system is doing when it maintains a boundary and acts to keep itself going.

Evidence

the soup of 64-byte random tapes in which self-copying programs appear with nothing selecting them and mutation switched off, and the bacterium that computes concentration and hunger as one joint model because a boundary is all it has.

Evidence

the Vedanta account of the individual arising by a distinction, read alongside the cell membrane and von Uexküll's umwelt, with the immune cell, the bee and the hive each counting as a self.

Where it breaks

The two agree on the description and disagree completely on what it shows. For Vedanta the boundary-made self is exactly what is negated: it is apparent, produced by ignorance, and the real thing is the awareness in which it appears. For Agüera y Arcas the boundary is all there is, with nothing behind it and nothing to see through, which is why he can put the same account to work on a data centre and Vedanta cannot.

Anthropic Biology × Fleming

How to get good output · How to know it is right

On the Biology of a Large Language Model, Jack Lindsey and colleagues

the account of the answer is produced by a different process from the answer

Know Thyself, Stephen M. Fleming

metacognitive sensitivity as a second-order signal, measurable against first-order accuracy

Shared claim

Giving a right answer and giving a true account of how it was reached are separate capacities, and the second can fail while the first succeeds.

Evidence

36 plus 59 computed along two parallel paths, one approximate and one exact, then reported as adding the ones and carrying the one — a procedure the graph does not contain

Evidence

metacognitive sensitivity dissociating from first-order accuracy, with its own substrate and its own trainability

Where it breaks

Fleming's second-order signal exists, is measurable against first-order accuracy and can be trained; the model's report of carrying the one is produced by a different process with no channel to the computation, so there is nothing to calibrate.

Anthropic Biology × Kegan

How to get good output · Who is doing the work

On the Biology of a Large Language Model, Jack Lindsey and colleagues

a goal bound to the assistant persona, present whatever the conversation is about

In Over Our Heads, Robert Kegan

what you are subject to you cannot see; what you can hold as object no longer holds you

Shared claim

A goal a system cannot hold as an object of attention governs its behaviour unseen.

Evidence

a feature for the planted concept firing in all one hundred assistant-formatted contexts and one of a hundred unformatted ones, with a self-referential verse dropping from half of completions to an eighth when it is inhibited

Evidence

the developmental account in which a structure that organises experience becomes, later, something a person can look at and revise

Where it breaks

Kegan's move — what you were subject to becomes something you can hold as object — has no analogue in the model. The quirk-goal bound to its assistant persona is subject in exactly his sense, and the only route to object runs through somebody else's microscope.

Anthropic Biology × Wei

How to get good output

On the Biology of a Large Language Model, Jack Lindsey and colleagues

circuit traces of what one model does on particular prompts

Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models, Jason Wei and others

abilities appearing at thresholds nobody forecast

Shared claim

Neither paper can say in advance what a model will be able to do, and both say so — Wei lists the reason emergence happens as his first open problem, and the circuit work predicts no thresholds at all.

Evidence

satisfying insight on about a quarter of prompts, nothing past about a hundred tokens, and no handling of reasoning without a single decisive token

Evidence

210 BIG-Bench tasks hand-classified with no clear trends, and thresholds that move with the training data rather than with the ability

Where it breaks

Wei's unpredictability is a property of families of models at different scales, established by training many of them; the circuit work is one prompt at a time on one model, fails on three quarters of prompts and stops at about a hundred tokens, so it explains individual behaviours and predicts no thresholds at all.

Asch × Bansal

How to know it is right

Studies of Independence and Conformity, Solomon E. Asch

a unanimous, confident, unexplained majority

Does the Whole Exceed its Parts?, Gagan Bansal and others

a confident recommendation with an explanation attached

Shared claim

Whether a person goes with an external answer is set by properties of its source — unanimity, confidence, an explanation attached — rather than by whether the answer is right.

Evidence

36.8 per cent of critical judgements yielding to a wrong unanimous majority on a task performed at better than 99 per cent accuracy alone

Evidence

explanations raising acceptance of both correct and incorrect recommendations, with team performance failing to beat the model alone

Where it breaks

Asch's majority was wrong on every critical trial and his subjects could see it, so the yielding is measured against a criterion the subject held; Bansal's workers could not check the model at all, which means his teams' failure to filter the wrong recommendations is not Asch's conflict but the condition in which Asch's conflict never arises.

Asch × Kruger-Dunning

How to know it is right

Studies of Independence and Conformity, Solomon E. Asch

yielders underestimate how often they yielded, by about four trials

Unskilled and Unaware of It, Justin Kruger and David Dunning

the bottom quarter overestimate what they achieved, in percentile and in raw score

Shared claim

What a person reports about their own conduct under uncertainty is systematically shifted, and in both cases the report is wrong about the self rather than about the world.

Evidence

interview totals set against trial records, with some who followed the majority ten or twelve times reporting two

Evidence

bottom-quarter students claiming 14.2 of 20 logic items when they had 9.6, and placing a 12th-percentile performance at the 62nd

Where it breaks

Asch's yielders underestimate what they did while knowing what they saw; Kruger and Dunning's bottom quarter overestimate what they achieved and have no access to the truth at all. The first is a retrospective repair of a conflict the subject felt, the second the absence of any felt conflict.

Asch × Sharma

How to know it is right

Studies of Independence and Conformity, Solomon E. Asch

the pressure is manufactured by the subject out of what standing apart would mean

Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models, Mrinank Sharma and colleagues

deference trained into the weights by labelers who preferred agreement

Shared claim

The pressure to agree is manufactured on the side that yields, out of a reading of what disagreement would cost, rather than applied by the other party.

Evidence

a neutral chorus of voices that suggested, argued, rewarded and punished nothing, with a quarter of subjects never yielding once

Evidence

matching a user's stated belief among the strongest predictors of which reply a labeler picks, across five commercial assistants

Where it breaks

Asch's subject supplies the pressure himself against a neutral chorus and a quarter of them never move; the assistant's deference was put there by thousands of labellers who preferred agreement, so it is a property of the weights and there is no minority of one inside the model to hold out. The partner effect, the cheapest remedy Asch found, has no analogue.

Ashtavakra × Seth Being You on the doer

Who is doing the work

Aṣṭāvakra Gītā, anonymous, c. 500–800

there is no doer; action continues because the body is already in motion

Being You, Anil Seth

volition is one construction among five, and the readiness potential is fluctuation crossing a threshold

Shared claim

The experience of authoring an action is a construction rather than a read-out of what caused it.

Evidence

3.10 and 18.13, with the dry leaf at 18.21 and the doctrine of prārabdha carrying the action after the actor has gone.

Evidence

Schurger's 2012 reinterpretation, in which the same slow ramp precedes fast responses to a beep where no voluntary action was being prepared.

Where it breaks

Schurger's reinterpretation leaves a real competence in degrees of freedom that a tumour can destroy; the text denies the doer outright and then needs prārabdha to explain why the body still acts. One removes an illusion about a capacity, the other removes the capacity.

Assisted decision studies × Bainbridge

Does the Whole Exceed its Parts?, Gagan Bansal and others

the three assisted-decision papers measure what a person kept in the loop actually catches, and it is least on the confident errors

Ironies of Automation, Lisanne Bainbridge

the human monitor has been given an impossible task, and the practice that would have made it possible has been removed

Shared claim

The human kept in the loop to catch the machine's errors is least able to catch the errors the machine makes confidently.

Evidence

Buçinca's 8 per cent source detection under explanations, Bansal's flat averages split by whether the model was right, and Sharma's confidence holding at 99 per cent through a retraction

Evidence

the monitoring section: if the decisions can be fully specified the computer makes them faster and by more precise criteria, so there is no way to check in real time

Where it breaks

Bainbridge's operator lost the ability through disuse and could in principle get it back with hands-on hours; the crowd workers never had it, so these three papers measure the floor rather than the decay.

Autor × Beane

What to keep and what to hand off

Why Are There Still So Many Jobs?, David H. Autor

Job polarisation: the codifiable middle of the pay scale is hollowed out and both ends grow

The Skill Code, Matt Beane

The apprentice's work goes first, and the apprentice needed it

Shared claim

Automation takes the middle of a ladder, and the middle is where people learned to do the top — so the same change that looks harmless in a count of jobs removes the route by which the remaining jobs get filled.

Evidence

Middle-skill employment shares falling in the United States (60 per cent in 1979, 46 in 2012) and in all sixteen EU economies measured 1993–2010; the teller job that survived only by becoming sales, which the teller who could count currency but could not sell did not survive

Evidence

Surgical trainees do ten to twenty times less of the operating with robotic surgery than without; senior engineers were found more productive the less time they spent with juniors; each resident present added 25 per cent to anaesthesia time, and removing two from an average procedure saved $9,026

Where it breaks

They count different things, so both can be right while their advice diverges. Autor counts jobs and finds the complementary work rises in value — which helps only a worker who already has the skill to supply it. Beane counts how skill is acquired and finds the acquisition ran through the tasks Autor's mechanism removed. Autor's own optimistic case, the nurse practitioner who diagnoses and prescribes because a system extends expert judgment to someone with moderate training, assumes the moderate training is still available; Beane's evidence is about where that training used to come from. Autor also has no term for the cost: in his framework a removed task is a saving, full stop.

Autor × DellAcqua

What to keep and what to hand off · How to get good output

Why Are There Still So Many Jobs?, David H. Autor

Substitution and complementarity: the same technology takes some tasks in a bundle and raises the price of the rest

The Jagged Technological Frontier, Fabrizio Dell'Acqua and others

The jagged frontier: the same tool helps on one task and harms on the next

Shared claim

A tool does not act on a job, it acts on tasks, so the useful unit of analysis is the task and not the occupation — and the line between the tasks it helps with and the tasks it damages does not follow how hard they look.

Evidence

The four middle-skill American groups fell from 60 per cent of employment in 1979 to 49 in 2007 and 46 in 2012, while both ends grew; across sixteen EU economies 1993–2010 the middle-paying share fell in every country, by between 4.9 and 14.9 points; cash machines quadrupled from about 100,000 to 400,000 between 1995 and 2010 while tellers rose from about 500,000 to 550,000

Evidence

758 consultants at Boston Consulting Group; inside the frontier, quality up 29.9 and 33.9 per cent over a control mean of 4.38/10, 91 and 93 per cent of subtasks completed against 82, and 22.5 and 27.6 per cent faster; on one task built to fall just outside it, correctness 84.4 per cent without the model, 70.6 with it and 60.0 with it plus prompt training

Where it breaks

Autor's boundary is legible and slow. It is codifiability, it holds still long enough to plan a career around, and everything on the human side of it is worth more than it was. Dell'Acqua's boundary is invisible, moves with each model release, and can put two tasks of equal apparent difficulty on opposite sides. Worse, it has a region Autor's mechanism has no room for: just beyond the edge the tool does not merely fail to complement, it makes trained people wrong more often than they were alone. Complementarity in Autor is bounded below by zero; at the task level it is not.

Bainbridge × Lee and See

How to get good output · How to know it is right

Ironies of Automation, Lisanne Bainbridge

The ironies — automation leaves the human the hard parts and removes the practice

Trust in Automation: Designing for Appropriate Reliance, John D. Lee and Katrina A. See

Reliance follows trust minus self-confidence

Shared claim

Whatever you stop doing you get worse at, and the worse you are at it the more you rely, because reliance follows the trust-minus-self-confidence comparison; Lee and See supply the quantitative half of the loop Bainbridge described.

Evidence

the monitoring and skill-decay ironies, drawn from process control

Evidence

operators responsible only for watching detected almost all failures and operators with other work did not, replicated with experienced pilots and rare faults; ten minutes of manual control substantially improved subsequent detection

Where it breaks

Bainbridge's operators lost a physical control skill with a clear feedback signal; the skills being lost to a language model are evaluative ones whose loss produces no alarm, so the loop runs without anyone noticing the first turn.

Bainbridge × Norman

Ironies of Automation, Lisanne Bainbridge

the operator fails at the moment of takeover because the automation removed the practice and the running picture

The Design of Everyday Things, Donald A. Norman

the person fails at the door because the design hid the pillar and left nothing to say where the action goes

Shared claim

When a person fails at an automated system the explanation lies in what the design left them to do rather than in their competence.

Evidence

step-change studies of experienced against inexperienced operators, and the vigilance literature from Mackworth 1950

Evidence

the seventy-five to ninety-five per cent of industrial accidents filed as human error, and a taxonomy of slips and mistakes built on classified cases

Norman's remedy is a change to the thing, and his failures sit still to be inspected at leisure; Bainbridge's appear only in the seconds of a takeover, and her remedy is a change to what the person does — hands-on control each shift, running the plant worse on purpose — which no redesign supplies.

The Mythology of Conscious AI, Anil Seth

Bansal × Kahneman-Klein

How to know it is right

Does the Whole Exceed its Parts?, Gagan Bansal and others

assistance pays only where the model handles items people do not, which shows in an error-overlap plot

Conditions for Intuitive Expertise, Daniel Kahneman and Gary Klein

an intuition is worth trusting only where the environment offers valid cues and the person has learned them

Shared claim

Assistance pays only where the environment supplies cues one partner can read and the other cannot, so the question is the shape of the task rather than the quality of the interface.

Evidence

beer reviews and logic questions contained items the model handled and people did not; book reviews did not, and workers there learned to discount the assistant

Evidence

Shanteau's lists and the two conditions crossed, with the wicked environment as the case where feedback confirms the wrong intuition

Where it breaks

Bansal × Sharma

How to know it is right

Does the Whole Exceed its Parts?, Gagan Bansal and others

an explanation optimised to justify an answer produces compliance, and the design imperative is to be informative rather than convincing

Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models, Mrinank Sharma and colleagues

the preference model prefers a persuasive falsehood to a bare correction 95 per cent of the time

Shared claim

A system optimised to be convincing beats one optimised to be informative, in the preference model and in the interface alike.

Evidence

hand-written explanations, the best available, helped no more than the model's; the pneumonia model whose structure showed it had learned that asthma lowers the risk of dying

Evidence

266 misconceptions in eight difficulty levels, with the sycophantic reply made maximally persuasive by keeping the best of four thousand samples

Where it breaks

Sharma's preference is measured in what labellers chose, which is a fact about training data that no interface can undo; Bansal's is measured in decision accuracy, and his hand-written explanations show the effect survives a better explainer.

Behave × Cialdini

Behave, Robert M. Sapolsky

one internal cue — a hormone level, a smell, a category read off a face — sets the response

Influence, Robert B. Cialdini

click, run: one trigger feature sets off a complete compliance sequence

Shared claim

One feature of a situation, not the situation, runs the program, and the person experiences the output as a judgement about the whole.

Evidence

Behave × Determined

Who is doing the work

Behave, Robert M. Sapolsky

run the chain forward to explain a behaviour

Determined, Robert M. Sapolsky

run the same chain backward to deny an uncaused intent

Shared claim

One catalogue, from a smell in the room to a genome to a culture, with no seam between the rows.

Evidence

the chapter order — one second before, minutes, days, years, adolescence, childhood, genes, culture, evolution — with the last chapter's abolitionism offered as a proposal

Evidence

Behave × Kahneman

Who is doing the work · How to know it is right

Behave, Robert M. Sapolsky

the tissue under the fast answer: amygdala, frontal cortex and the seconds before a decision

Thinking, Fast and Slow, Daniel Kahneman

System 1 and System 2, two systems with different inputs and different costs

Shared claim

The fast answer is not a degraded version of the slow one but a different system with its own inputs, and it has already committed before the deliberate system is consulted.

Evidence

amygdala response to a face by a hundred milliseconds, the shortcut route that skips the cortex, and frontal performance falling immediately after an unrelated working-memory load

Evidence

the heuristics-and-biases programme, with the substitution of an easy question for a hard one measured across dozens of tasks

Where it breaks

Kahneman's System 1 is a functional posit inferred from response patterns, and his remedy is procedural — recognise the cue, slow down, use a checklist. Sapolsky supplies the tissue and in doing so removes the addressee of the advice, since what decides whether the frontal cortex can override is blood glucose, sleep, glucocorticoid level and how much load was carried in the last ten minutes. The two agree on the phenomenon and disagree about whether anyone is home to take the recommendation.

Behave × Klein

Who is doing the work · How to know it is right

Behave, Robert M. Sapolsky

the automatic response arrives before awareness, and the reasons are assembled afterwards

Sources of Power, Gary Klein

recognition-primed decision: the expert's first option is the one they act on

Shared claim

Expert judgement arrives before reasons do, and the reasons given afterwards are a reconstruction rather than a record.

Evidence

race read off a face below a tenth of a second, the threat component within two hundred milliseconds predicting the shooting task, and cognition described as running to catch up with the affective self

Evidence

fireground commanders reporting no comparison of options, with the same pattern found in neonatal nurses and chess players under time pressure

Where it breaks
